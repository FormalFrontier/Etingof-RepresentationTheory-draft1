**`Chapter2/Problem2_8_11.lean`**, new `HilbertSeries` section (sorry-free, axiom-clean, works over
any `CommRing k`):

- `adjacencyPS k Q` : the adjacency matrix pushed into `Matrix Q Q k⟦t⟧` via `PowerSeries.C`, so
  that `X • adjacencyPS k Q` is literally the matrix `t M_Q`.
- `resolvent k Q` : the matrix of generating functions, `resolvent i j = ∑ₙ (M_Q^n) i j tⁿ`.
- `coeff_adjacencyPS_mul_resolvent` : multiplying by `M_Q` shifts the path count by one,
  `coeff d ((M_Q · resolvent) i j) = (M_Q^(d+1)) i j`. This is `pow_succ'` plus `coeff_C_mul`.
- `one_sub_smul_adjacencyPS_mul_resolvent` : the geometric-series identity
  `(I - t M_Q) · resolvent = I`. Coefficientwise: at `t⁰` the `X`-multiplied term vanishes and
  `M_Q^0 = I`; at `t^(e+1)` the two path counts cancel.
- `isUnit_det_one_sub_smul_adjacencyPS`, `inv_one_sub_smul_adjacencyPS`,
  `resolvent_mul_one_sub_smul_adjacencyPS` : `I - t M_Q` is genuinely invertible over `k⟦t⟧` and
  `Matrix.inv` of it *is* the resolvent. This matters: without it, the `(I - t M_Q)⁻¹` in the
  headline could be `Matrix.inv`'s junk value and the theorem would be vacuous.
- `one_vecMul_dotProduct_one` : `𝟙ᵀ A 𝟙 = ∑_{i,j} A i j`, so the statement can be written in the
  book's `𝟙ᵀ … 𝟙` shape rather than as a double sum.
- **`hilbertSeries_pathAlgebra`** (the headline):
  `mk (fun n => #{paths of length n in Q}) = 𝟙ᵀ (I - t M_Q)⁻¹ 𝟙` as an identity in `k⟦t⟧`.
- `hilbertSeries_pathAlgebra_adjacencyMatrix` : the same identity with the coefficient written as
  `𝟙ᵀ M_Q^n 𝟙`, i.e. the book's `∑ₙ (𝟙ᵀ M_Q^n 𝟙) tⁿ = 𝟙ᵀ (I - t M_Q)⁻¹ 𝟙`.
