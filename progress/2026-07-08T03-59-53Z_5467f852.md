## Accomplished

Proved **Problem 3.3.3(a)** (`simpleModule_prod_iff`) in
`EtingofRepresentationTheory/Chapter3/Problem3_3_3.lean` — issue #5976.

`A = ∀ i, 𝒜 i` a finite product of rings, `V` an `A`-module. The theorem: `V` is
simple iff the summand `1ᵢ V = LinearMap.range (idemProj i)` is simple for exactly
one `i` while `1ⱼ V = ⊥` for all other `j`.

Added supporting lemmas (pure ring/module theory, no base field):
- `single_mul_single_eq`: orthogonality `1ᵢ · 1ⱼ = δᵢⱼ 1ᵢ`.
- `sum_single_one`: `∑ᵢ 1ᵢ = 1` (via `Finset.univ_sum_single`).
- `single_smul_single_smul`: `1ᵢ · (1ⱼ · v) = δᵢⱼ (1ᵢ · v)`.
- `sum_single_smul`: `∑ᵢ 1ᵢ · v = v`.
- `mem_range_idemProj`: `v ∈ 1ᵢ V ↔ 1ᵢ · v = v`.
- `range_eq_top_iff` / `range_eq_bot_iff`: the summand is `⊤`/`⊥` iff `1ᵢ` acts as
  identity/zero.

Proof of the main iff:
- (⇒) `V` simple ⇒ each `1ₖ V` is `⊥` or `⊤` (`eq_bot_or_eq_top`); not all `⊥`
  (they sum to `V`, `V` nontrivial); at most one `⊤` (orthogonality forces
  `v = 0`). The unique `⊤` summand is `≅ V` (via `Submodule.topEquiv` and
  `LinearEquiv.isSimpleModule_iff`), hence simple.
- (⇐) all other summands `⊥` ⇒ `1ᵢ` acts as identity ⇒ `1ᵢ V = ⊤ ≅ V`, so `V` is
  simple.
