## Key findings (route analysis)
1. **Per-summand vanishing is FALSE** (refutes the prior skip comment's "one
   line" plan). For λ=(2,1), T=[[0,1],[2]], the standard polytabloid `e_T` has
   coefficient −1 at the tabloid `{{1,2},{0}}`, which is NOT
   column-standardizable. So `e_T` (and likewise `f_w(σ)`) does **not** vanish at
   every non-standardizable tabloid; `X(t₁)=0` must come from genuine
   cross-cancellation between `f_w(σ)` and `M`. Brute-forced in
   `progress/r2b-fact2-persummand-refuted.py`.
2. **The standard-basis route is circular.** "Dominance-maximal support tabloid
   of an element of `S^λ` is column-standard" needs `X ∈ S^λ`, i.e.
   `f_w(σ) ∈ V` — the downstream goal the leading-term elimination proves by
   induction. (And `f_w(σ)` is not obviously a single polytabloid: its term is
   `[w·q⁻¹·σ]`, not `[q⁻¹·(wσ)]`.) Assuming membership is circular.
3. **Two-source obstruction is real.** A single global column-swap involution
   `q ↦ q·τ` (τ ∈ Q_λ) preserves the `f_w` fibre `[w q⁻¹ σ]` iff `wτw⁻¹ ∈ P_λ`,
   but preserves an `e_T` fibre `[q⁻¹ σ_T]` iff `τ ∈ P_λ` — impossible for a
   nontrivial column swap. So a single involution cannot cancel both sources;
   the required construction is genuinely the "novel two-source involution" the
   prior session named, and the Garnir-straightening-raises-dominance
   infrastructure it needs does **not** exist in the codebase (only the easy
   `garnirAnnihilate_tabloid`, which handles same-row pairs).
