Proof strategy (row-length characterisation, using the corner infrastructure already in
`FRTHelpers.lean`):

- Helper `mem_cells_iff_getD`: `(i,j) ∈ la.toYoungDiagram.cells ↔ j < la.sortedParts.getD i 0`.
- Helper `isOuterCorner_iff`: `IsOuterCorner i j ↔ j+1 = L[i] ∧ L[i+1] < L[i]` (L = sorted parts).
- Helper `content_removeOuterCorner`: removing an outer corner drops content by `col − row`
  (via `Finset.sum_erase_eq_sub` and `toYoungDiagram_removeOuterCorner`).
- Helper `removeOuterCorner_mem_removeSquare`: corner deletion lands in `removeSquare`.
- Helper `removeSquare_content`: every `ν ∈ removeSquare la` deletes exactly one outer corner
  `d` (the unique cell of `la.cells \ ν.cells`, a singleton by cardinality `m+1` vs `m`) and
  `content ν = content la − (col(d) − row(d))`.
- Main iff:
  - (⟸) rectangular ⇒ `L` constant ⇒ the removed corner is forced to be the bottom corner
    `(r−1, cval−1)`, so all `ν` share content.
  - (⟹) contrapositive: not rectangular ⇒ an interior strict descent exists ⇒ two outer
    corners with strictly different `col − row`, giving two `removeSquare` elements of
    different content (via `Int.cast_injective` on the ℂ equations).

No representation theory involved; pure `Nat.Partition`/`YoungDiagram` combinatorics.
