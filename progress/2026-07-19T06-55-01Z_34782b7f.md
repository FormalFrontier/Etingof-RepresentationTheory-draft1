The crux was a factor `k`-module diamond: the statement's `ModuleCat.of (A₁⊗A₂)(M₁⊗M₂)` builds
`M₁ ⊗[k] M₂` from the ambient `Module k Mᵢ`, whereas `extTensorFunctorLeftObj` builds it from the
restricted `Module.compHom Mᵢ (algebraMap k Aᵢ)` (`restrictModule₁L`/`restrictModule₂L`). These
`Module k Mᵢ` instances are propositionally — not definitionally — equal, so the two carriers
`M₁ ⊗[k] M₂` are genuinely different types. The proof:

1. `e1/e2 : (inferInstance : Module k Mᵢ) = Module.compHom Mᵢ (algebraMap k Aᵢ)` via `Module.ext'`
   with `IsScalarTower.algebraMap_smul`;
2. `subst e1 e2` replaces the ambient factor `k`-structures with the restricted ones, making the two
   carriers **definitionally** equal;
3. `letI` (not `haveI`) re-registers the restricted `Module k Mᵢ` transparently, so typeclass
   resolution feeds `instModule_eq_extTensorModuleLeft` an instance defeq to the `Module.compHom`
   term baked into `instM`'s carrier by `subst` (an opaque `haveI` fails the defeq check here);
4. `rw [instModule_eq_extTensorModuleLeft instM hM]` matches the `A₁⊗A₂`-action, then `rfl`
   (default transparency, unfolding the non-reducible `extTensorFunctorLeftObj`) closes it.
