### The calculus

The issue's suggested route was to chase structure constants layer by layer. That is not what the
file does. Instead everything is generated by one identity,

`⁅aᵢ, ⁅ȳ, u⁆⁆ = ⁅ȳ, ⁅aᵢ, u⁆⁆ - ⁅aᵢ₊₁, u⁆`  (`lie_aElt_lie_yb`),

which iterates to the binomial expansion

`⁅aᵢ, Dʲu⁆ = Σₗ (-1)ˡ (j choose l) Dʲ⁻ˡ ⁅aᵢ₊ₗ, u⁆`  (`lie_aElt_dY_two` … `lie_aElt_dY_five`).

This is what makes the induction tractable: the closure condition of
`span_eq_top_of_closed_under_ad` is about `⁅x̄, Dⁱ u⁆` all the way along a string, and the
expansion converts it into a *finite table* of the five values `⁅aᵢ, u⁆`, `i ≤ 4`, at the top of
the string. So the layer invariants (`OddLayer`, `EvenLayer`) are stated as tables, not as
`ad(x̄)`-formulas, and the induction step becomes bookkeeping with the expansion plus two
commutation facts.

The two Serre relations enter in exactly two places, both isolated as named lemmas:

* `serre_x` — `ad(x̄)² D = 2 ad(x̄) D ad(x̄) - D ad(x̄)²`, from `⁅a₀, a₁⁆ = 0`;
* `serre_y` — the `j = 5` expansion combined with `a₅ = 0`. This is the *only* place
  `ad(y)⁵(x) = 0` is used in the induction.

### The step

`EvenLayer.of_oddLayer` (hypotheses `2 ≠ 0`, `5 ≠ 0`) proves the three even-layer fields from the
six odd-layer ones:

* `⁅x̄, c⁆ = 0` — `serre_x` applied to `D²b`;
* `D³c = 0` — `serre_y` applied to `D b`, which produces `10 · D³c = 0`. This is where the
  characteristic hypotheses come from, and it is the reason the even layers have length `3`
  rather than `5`;
* `2⁅a₂,c⁆ = 3 D⁅a₁,c⁆` — `ad(a₂)` commutes with `ad(x̄)`, giving `⁅a₂,c⁆ = -3⁅x̄, D²c⁆`; expanding
  `⁅x̄, D²c⁆` once more turns this into `4⁅a₂,c⁆ = 6 D⁅a₁,c⁆`.

`oddLayer_xb` checks the invariant for the layer of `t`-degree `1` (`b = x̄`, next top
`c = -⁅a₀,a₃⁆`, using `lie_a1_a3_add` for the `⁅a₄,b⁆ = 2Dc` field), so the step is not vacuous.
`evenLayer_deg_two`, `dY_three_lie_a0_a3` (`D³⁅a₀,a₃⁆ = 0`, the new content) and
`lie_zero_deg_two` (agreeing with the existing `lie_a0_lie_a0_a3`) are the resulting statements
about the degree-`2` layer.

### Worth recording

**The tautology trap.** Most "obvious" routes to a layer identity are consequences of the
commutation relations `[D, Aᵢ] = Aᵢ₊₁`, `[X, A₁] = [X, A₂] = 0` alone, and those are automatically
consistent — so they reduce to `0 = 0`. Any genuine new fact has to consume either `a₅ = 0` in the
`D^a X D^b` form (`serre_y`) or a bracket that is not determined by the current table. Several
hours of paper work went into distinguishing the two; the derivations that do work are recorded in
#7749 so the next agent does not repeat the search.
