- **Statement correction (required, faithful to the book):** added `[IsAlgClosed k]`. Without it
  the claim "dim = p" is false (over a non-closed field the Weyl algebra is Azumaya of rank p² over
  its center and simple modules can have dimension p·d). The book's part (c) hint ("let v be an
  eigenvector of y") only makes sense over an algebraically closed field. Documented in the docstring.
- **Proof (following the book):**
  - `central_smul_scalar` (new private lemma): a central element acts as a k-scalar on a simple
    finite-dimensional module over an algebraically closed field. Proved via eigenvalue existence
    (`Module.End.exists_eigenvalue`) + the eigenspace being a nonzero invariant submodule, hence ⊤
    by simplicity — this is Schur's lemma without needing the "End is a division ring" packaging.
  - `mem_of_invariant` (new private lemma): a nonzero k-submodule stable under the whole algebra
    action equals V (the "nonzero invariant submodule of a simple module is everything" step).
  - Main argument: eigenvector v of y with eigenvalue lam; lowering operator N = ρ(y) − lam gives
    `N (xⁱv) = i·xⁱ⁻¹v`, so `Nⁱ(xⁱv) = i!·v` and `N^(i+1)(xⁱv)=0`. Linear independence of
    {v, xv, …, x^(p-1)v} follows by `Fin.snoc` induction (a vector in the span is killed by Nᵐ but
    the new top vector is not, since m! ≠ 0 mod p). x^p acting as scalar μ makes the k-span of the p
    vectors invariant under x and y, hence (via free-algebra `adjoin_induction`) under the whole
    algebra; being nonzero it is all of V. So the p vectors are a basis and `finrank = p`.
