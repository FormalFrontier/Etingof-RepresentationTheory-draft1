## Accomplished

Resolved fidelity issue #5614 (Chapter3/Theorem3.10.2 — dropped uniqueness of V ⊗ W).

Etingof Theorem 3.10.2(ii) asserts that an irreducible representation `M` of `A ⊗ B`
has the form `V ⊗ W` for **unique** `V` and `W`. The existing Lean theorem
`Etingof.tensor_product_irreducible_classification` proved only existence (a bare `∃ V W`).
I added the missing uniqueness as a companion theorem and supporting lemmas in
`EtingofRepresentationTheory/Chapter3/Theorem3_10_2.lean`:

- `Etingof.tensor_product_irreducible_classification_unique`: for any two `A`-`B`-equivariant
  factorizations of the same module, packaged as an equivalence `e : V ⊗ W ≃ V' ⊗ W'`, we get
  `Nonempty (V ≃ₗ[A] V') ∧ Nonempty (W ≃ₗ[B] W')` — V and W are unique up to isomorphism.
- `Etingof.tensor_left_factor_unique`: the left-factor half, proved via Schur's lemma
  (`LinearMap.bijective_of_ne_zero`): a contraction `v ↦ rid (map id ψ (e (v ⊗ w₀)))` is a
  nonzero `A`-linear map between the simple modules `V`, `V'`, hence an isomorphism.
- Helpers: `exists_functional_ne_zero`, `tmul_ne_zero`, `exists_contraction_ne_zero`,
  `lid_map_eq_apply_rid`, `rid_mapψ_lsmul`, `comm_map_apply` (factor-swap naturality, used to
  derive the `W`-factor uniqueness by symmetry).

No `sorry`, no `native_decide`. The `W`-factor case reuses the left-factor lemma applied to
the factor-swapped equivalence `W ⊗ V ≃ W' ⊗ V'`.
