The previous statement only proved the single-factor uniqueness: a finite-dimensional simple
`Mat_d(k)`-module is isomorphic to the standard rep `Fin d → k`. The book's theorem is about
`A = ⊕ᵢ Mat_{dᵢ}(k)`. I rewrote the theorem to assert the full book claim and proved it
sorry-free:

* (1) each standard rep `Vⱼ = k^{dⱼ}` is a simple `A`-module (`isSimpleModule_vModuleProd`);
* (2) every finite-dimensional simple `A`-module is isomorphic to some `Vⱼ`
  (`exists_iso_vModuleProd`);
* (3) every `A`-module is semisimple, i.e. an internal direct sum of simple submodules
  (`isSemisimpleModule_of_matrixProd`), which by (1)-(2) are copies of the `Vⱼ`.

Key techniques:
* `A = ⊕ᵢ Mat_{dᵢ}(k)` modeled as the finite product ring `∀ i, Matrix (Fin (dᵢ)) (Fin (dᵢ)) k`;
  Mathlib gives `IsSemisimpleRing (Π i, Rᵢ)` for free, so Part 3 is `inferInstance`.
* Standard rep `Vⱼ` made an `A`-module via `Module.compHom` along `Pi.evalRingHom`; simplicity
  transferred from the single-factor result with `LinearMap.isSimpleModule_iff_of_bijective`.
* Classification (Part 2): used the central idempotents `eᵢ = Pi.single i 1`. For a simple `W`,
  the `A`-linear idempotent `μ : w ↦ eᵢ • w` has `range μ = ⊤` for some `i`, forcing `eᵢ` to act
  as the identity; the `A`-action then factors through the `i`-th projection, giving `W` a
  `Mat_{dᵢ}(k)`-module structure to which the single-factor classification applies. The resulting
  `Mat_{dᵢ}`-linear iso is upgraded to `A`-linearity.
