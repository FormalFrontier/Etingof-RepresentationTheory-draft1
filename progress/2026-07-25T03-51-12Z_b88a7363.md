* `Etingof.PIDDecomposition A M` bundles a decomposition of an `A`-module: free rank, a finite
  index type with decidable equality for the cyclic summands, their generators `gen i`, and the
  plain isomorphism `M ≃ₗ[A] (Fin n → A) × ((i : ι) → A ⧸ Ideal.span {gen i})` (the form the
  `Tor` second-argument maps need).
* `Etingof.exists_pidDecomposition` derives one from `Module.equiv_free_prod_directSum` for any
  finitely generated module over a PID; `exists_pidDecomposition_int` and
  `exists_pidDecomposition_polynomial` are the `ℤ` and `k[X]` instantiations.
* `PIDDecomposition.summand : Fin n ⊕ ι → ModuleCat A` is the single summand family (free
  summands `A` on the left, cyclic `A ⧸ (gen i)` on the right); `equivDirectSum` and
  `biproductIso : ModuleCat.of A M ≅ biproduct D.summand` are the `DirectSum` and categorical
  forms (the shape `Ext` consumes).
* `PIDDecomposition.mopSummand` / `mopBiproductIso : mopOf A M ≅ biproduct D.mopSummand` are the
  right-module versions in `ModuleCat Aᵐᵒᵖ` (the shape the first argument of `Tor` consumes).
* Bridges to the shapes `Problem8_2_7.lean` states its building blocks for: `intCyclicIso`
  (`ℤ ⧸ (a) ≅ ZMod a`), `mopIntCyclicIso`, `mopSelfIso` (`mopOf A A ≅` right regular `A`),
  `mopPolyCyclicIso`.
* Worked examples `exampleIntProdZMod4` (`ℤ × ZMod 4`) and `exampleZModSix` (`ZMod 6`), plus
  `example`s exercising every derived form and bridge on them.

## Current frontier

Key design decisions a successor should know:

* **Everything is proved for a general PID**, not separately for `ℤ` and `k[X]`; the two cases of
  Problem 8.2.7 are instantiations. That is why the file has no `ℤ`/`k[X]` duplication.
* **The `Aᵐᵒᵖ` side goes through restriction of scalars, not by hand.** `Etingof.mopFunctor A =
  ModuleCat.restrictScalars (mopRingEquiv A).toRingHom` is an additive equivalence, so
  `Functor.mapBiproduct` transports the whole biproduct decomposition in one step. Trying to
  transport a plain `DirectSum` isomorphism instead does *not* work smoothly: for a `Sum.elim`
  family the pointwise `Module Aᵐᵒᵖ` instances are a case split, so `⨁ j, (summand j)` over `A`
  and over `Aᵐᵒᵖ` are not definitionally the same type. Working with `ModuleCat`-valued families
  (instances bundled in the objects) is what makes the whole file go through.
* `mopOf A M` is *definitionally* the object `Problem8_2_7.lean` builds with its `local instance`s
  `mopZMod` / `mopPolyQuot` (`Etingof.mopOf_eq_of_compHom` is `rfl`), so the bridges re-enable
  those instances with `attribute [local instance] mopZMod mopPolyQuot` and land on exactly the
  objects `Problem_8_2_7_i_tor_*` / `Problem_8_2_7_ii_tor_*` talk about.
* The free summands of `mopSummand` are `mopOf A A`, not Mathlib's right regular
  `Semiring.toOppositeModule` module; the two are *not* definitionally equal (`op a • x = a * x`
  versus `x * a`), so `mopSelfIso` is the required conversion before applying
  `Problem_8_2_7_i_tor_free_vanish`.
