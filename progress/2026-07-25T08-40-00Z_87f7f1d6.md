### Two things worth recording

**The induction does not need to carry the pair `(b, c)`.** #7732 and #7749 both assert that
`⁅a₁, b'⁆ = 0` is not a consequence of `EvenLayer c` alone and has to come from `⁅⁅a₁,a₄⁆, b⁆`
with `b` the previous odd top. That is wrong. `serre_x` (the *first* Serre relation in operator
form) applied to `D c` gives `3⁅a₁, b'⁆ = 0` directly; applied to `D² c` it then gives
`2⁅a₂, b'⁆ = 0`; and `serre_y` applied to `D c` gives `3 D⁵b' = 0`. So the invariant is a plain
`EvenLayer`, and `EvenLayer.step` closes a *two-degree* induction with no odd-layer input at all.
This is why `evenTower` exists and why #7750's deliverables can be re-read with a single-argument
invariant.

**The one open field is exactly a centre statement.** `serre_y` at `b'` gives
`D(⁅a₄,b'⁆ - 2Dc'') = 0` (already known from #7732), and — this is new — `serre_x` at `D³b'` gives
`⁅x̄, ⁅a₄,b'⁆⁆ = -2⁅a₁, c''⁆`, i.e. `ad(x̄)` also kills the defect. The elements killing a fixed `w`
form a Lie subalgebra, so `eq_top_of_closed_under_ad` upgrades those two vanishings to
"`defect c` is central". That converts an open *computation* (find `⁅⁅a₁,a₄⁆, c⁆`) into a single
clean *statement* (`Z(𝔤₄) = 0`), filed as #7753.

Dead ends checked and recorded in #7753 so no successor repeats them: `serre_y` applied to `c`,
`D c`, `D²c`, `b'`, `Db'`, `D²b'`, `D⁴b'` and `serre_x` applied to every `Dʲc` and `Dʲb'`. The
only ones carrying new information are the six listed above; `serre_x` at `D⁴b'` yields the third
`EvenLayer` field for `c''` rather than anything about the defect.
