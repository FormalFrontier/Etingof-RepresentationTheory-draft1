`Chapter6/Example6_2_4_Sink.lean` introduces the second orientation `• → • ← •`, absent from
the project until now: the structure `A₃SinkRep` (spaces `V₁ →f V₂ ←g V₃`), its
`Indecomposable` predicate, the dimension-vector theorem `Etingof.Example_6_2_4_sink`, and the
classification `A₃SinkRep.exists_unique_iso_rep`. The book's argument is followed directly.
Splitting off `ker f` and `ker g` shows both maps are injective once `V₂ ≠ 0`; splitting off a
complement of `range f ⊔ range g` shows the two images span. When `range f ⊓ range g = ⊥` the
images are complementary and the representation splits off an outer vertex; otherwise the
decomposition

  `(f⁻¹D, D, g⁻¹D) ⊕ (Q₁, f Q₁ ⊕ g Q₃, Q₃)`,  `D = range f ⊓ range g`,

forces `D = V₂`, so both maps are isomorphisms. Each of the six representatives is constructed
and proved indecomposable, and the six dimension vectors are distinct, which gives uniqueness.

## Notes for successors

A representative with a `PUnit` vertex has a floating universe, and mentioning it in a
*hoisted* term (`obtain ⟨e⟩ := nonempty_linearEquiv_of_finrank_eq … (M' := (rep_110 k).V₁)`)
creates a fresh copy of the representative whose other `PUnit` universes are unconstrained. The
declaration then fails with "contains universe level metavariables" pointing at the *body*, not
at the offending line. Two fixes, both used here: pin the target as the concrete type
(`(M' := k)`) when it is the field, and leave the `PUnit`-valued components as `?_` holes in the
structure literal so their type comes from the goal. Pinning `(M' := (rep k).Vᵢ)` *inside* a
structure literal instead makes `Module.Free k (rep_100 k).V₂` time out at 20000 synthesis
heartbeats, so the hoist-or-hole choice matters in both directions.

The `Indecomposable` disjunction is `(p₁ = ⊥ ∧ p₂ = ⊥ ∧ p₃ = ⊥) ∨ (q₁ = ⊥ ∧ q₂ = ⊥ ∧ q₃ = ⊥)`,
so `refine ⟨…, ?_, ?_, …⟩` on the final goal makes each dimension an independent goal and the
later ones cannot use the earlier ones. Prove `finrank V₂ = 1` as a `have` first, then supply the
tuple in one `exact`.
