## Decisions made

**Why no rearrangement can close #7783, with a reason.** `ker (gbar k)` is a central ideal
concentrated in the imaginary bidegrees, so `0 → ker gbar → 𝔤₄ → L → 0` is a graded central
extension of the loop model. Jacobi identities hold in *any* central extension, so bracket
manipulation inside `𝔤₄` cannot distinguish split from non-split; the obstruction is a
cohomology class. Via Hopf's formula (centrality gives `⁅F, R⁆ ⊆ I`),

    ker (𝔤₄ ↠ L) ≅ H₂(L) / ⟨[ad(x)²y], [ad(y)⁵x]⟩,

and the two relator classes sit in bidegrees `(2,1)` and `(1,5)`, off the imaginary ray, so on
that ray the kernel *is* `H₂(L)`. Garland-Lepowsky gives `H₂(𝔫₊) = ⊕_{ℓ(w)=2} k_{ρ-wρ}`; in
rank 2 the two length-2 Weyl elements give exactly `2α₁+α₂ = (2,1)` and `α₁+5α₂ = (1,5)` —
the two Serre relators and nothing else. So `H²(L,k)` is 2-dimensional in total and the
statement to be proved is true.

**One further route ruled out this session** (recorded on #7783 and #7803): the generation
argument. `𝔤₄_β = ⁅𝔤₄_{β-(1,0)}, x̄⁆ + ⁅𝔤₄_{β-(0,1)}, ȳ⁆`; for imaginary `β` both summands are
1-dimensional and the first is `⁅x̄, D⁴(topOdd m)⁆`, i.e. the defect relation itself. Bound `2`
again.

**Route chosen for the successors:** the central-extension / 2-cocycle route, in preference to
both routes suggested in #7783. `L` is already fully explicit here (`loopPos`, `loopVec`,
`linearIndependent_loopVec`, `range_matHom₄_eq_loopPos`) and every weight space of `L` is
one-dimensional, so the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex in a fixed weight is purely combinatorial.
Gröbner-Shirshov needs a composition-diamond lemma Mathlib lacks; the Verma/Casimir route needs
`𝔥`, weight modules and integrability the project lacks. The Casimir route is recorded on #7803
as the fallback.
