## Accomplished

Proved **Problem 4.1.4** (issue #5971): every irreducible representation of a
`p`-group over a field of characteristic `p` is trivial. Discharged the single
proof `sorry` in `EtingofRepresentationTheory/Chapter4/Problem4_1_4.lean`.

The mathematical crux — the char-`p` fixed-vector theorem for `p`-groups — is not
in Mathlib, so it is proved here as `Etingof.Problem4_1_4.exists_fixed_vector` by
strong induction on `Nat.card G`:

- A nontrivial `p`-group has a nontrivial center (`IsPGroup.center_nontrivial`);
  pick a nontrivial central `z`.
- `ρ z - 1` is nilpotent: `(ρ z)^{orderOf z} = 1`, `orderOf z = p^j`, and
  `(x - 1)^{p^j} = x^{p^j} - 1` in characteristic `p`
  (`sub_pow_expChar_pow_of_commute`, with `CharP (Module.End k V) p` from
  `Module.charP_end`). A nilpotent endomorphism of a nonzero module has nonzero
  kernel `W = ker (ρ z - 1)`.
- `W` is a `G`-subrepresentation (`Representation.subrepresentation`) on which `z`
  (hence `⟨z⟩ = zpowers z`, normal since `z` central) acts trivially, so the action
  factors through the smaller `p`-group `G ⧸ ⟨z⟩` (`Representation.ofQuotient`).
  Apply the induction hypothesis there and pull the fixed vector back to `V`.

The main theorem then follows: a nonzero fixed vector generates the whole simple
`k[G]`-module (`IsSimpleModule.span_singleton_eq_top`), and `ρ.asAlgebraHom r v` is
always a `k`-scalar multiple of `v`, forcing `ρ g = id` for every `g`.
