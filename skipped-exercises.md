2.7.5 — q-Weyl algebra. Determine the center and two-sided ideals of the q-deformed Weyl algebra for all q, then classify its finite-dimensional irreducibles (which only exist when q is a root of unity). Hard because it needs a full case analysis on the arithmetic of q via determinant/root-of-unity arguments.

2.16.3 — Free-ish Lie algebras gₙ. For the Lie algebra generated by x, y with relations ad(x)²y = ad(y)ⁿ⁺¹x = 0, compute dim g₁, g₂, g₃ and show g₄ is infinite-dimensional with an explicit basis. The finite cases are combinatorial bookkeeping; the g₄ infiniteness proof is genuinely delicate.

2.16.5 — Quantum sl₂. Classify all irreducible representations of the quantum group U_q(sl₂), treating the root-of-unity and non-root-of-unity cases separately. It's a large quantum-algebra project with two structurally different regimes (finite vs. infinite-order K).

4.12.8 — Finite subgroups of SO(3)/SU(2). Derive the classification of finite rotation groups (cyclic, dihedral, A₄, S₄, A₅) via the pole-counting equation 2(1−1/n) = Σ(1−1/mᵢ), then lift it to SU(2). The counting argument is elegant but the full case enumeration and the SU(2) double-cover lift are long.

5.24.2 — Invariants of matrix tuples. Show the ring of simultaneous-conjugation invariants of k-tuples of N×N matrices is generated by trace-of-word functions T_w. Requires realizing the invariant space as a symmetric-power tensor and applying Schur-Weyl duality — heavy infrastructure.

6.1.3 — Dynkin diagrams (+ E7/E8, +tilde-E continuations). Classify which connected graphs have 2I−R positive definite (finite ADE Dynkin) versus positive semidefinite (affine), including the exceptional E7/E8 and the affine tilde-E cases. This is the combinatorial heart of the ADE classification and the densest single problem in the book.

6.1.6 — McKay correspondence. For a finite G ⊂ SU(2), show the McKay graph (vertices = irreps, edges = multiplicities in V⊗Vⱼ) is a connected affine Dynkin diagram, and read off the irrep dimensions from the vertex labels. It depends on 6.1.3 and 4.12.8 and ties character theory to the ADE classification — very hard and highly dependent.

8.2.8 — Künneth for Tor/Ext. Prove Tor and Ext of tensor-product modules over A₁⊗A₂ decompose as the graded sum of tensor products of the factor Tor/Ext groups. This is a full Künneth-formula argument in homological algebra, needing spectral-sequence or double-complex machinery not yet in the project.

9.6.5 — Morita-type equivalence. Show the functors F = Hom(P, −) and G = P ⊗_B − are quasi-inverse, hence equivalences, using projectivity of P and a short-exact-sequence/exactness argument. It's an abstract category-theory capstone with several interlocking naturality and exactness obligations.

Out-of-scope — 2.13.1 (Dehn invariant / Hilbert's 3rd). Prove a cube and regular tetrahedron of equal volume are not scissors-congruent, via additivity of the Dehn invariant plus irrationality of arccos(1/3)/π. Only part (b), the irrationality, is tractable; the geometric scissors-congruence parts (a)/(c) are far outside Mathlib's current reach, so I flagged the whole problem out-of-scope.
